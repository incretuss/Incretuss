HeatMap Builder performs the calculation stage of temperature mapping and 
qualification of storage areas, chambers and temperature-controlled 
equipment. The software merges files from BEREG and TR series data loggers, 
calculates metrics for each point, assesses the risk level relative to the range 
limits, builds charts and temperature and humidity maps on the room plan, 
and exports the results for the mapping report – including a single combined 
Excel table for calculations under the customer’s own methodology. The 
customer defines the methodology and acceptance criteria; analysis, 
conclusions and the report are prepared by the customer’s specialist.

2. Place of the software in the mapping procedure
The Guideline on Temperature Mapping of Medicinal Product Storage Areas (EEC Board Recommendation No. 8 of 
20 April 2026) divides the procedure into four stages: preparation of the protocol, performance of mapping, 
preparation of the report, and corrective and other actions based on the report’s recommendations (clause 8). 
HeatMap Builder works between the second and third stages: it receives logger data after the study and prepares 
the calculation part of the report.

In the software, critical points are the points with a high or medium risk of exceeding the range limits. Points with 
the largest temperature fluctuations – critical points in the Guideline’s terms – are identified by the maximum 
variation and the variation histogram.
Mapping results are used to determine the locations of routine monitoring sensors (clause 19). For continuous 
monitoring at these points the manufacturer produces the GIGROTERMON system.
3. Software functions
3.1 Loading and merging data
In a single run the software accepts up to 1000 files and merges them into one model. BEREG logger files are read 
in the binary BEREG format – a byte-for-byte image of the instrument memory – or in CSV format; TR series logger 
data are read in CSV format. From files of the multichannel Bereg-RP and Bereg-RK data loggers the temperature 
and relative humidity channels are processed; files with a single temperature channel are processed as standard.

3.2 Point identification
Each data row is linked to the logger identification number and to a point number of up to 3 characters, in digits or 
Latin characters. The point number labels the point on charts, maps and in exports. The software takes the label 
that the user has assigned to a BEREG data logger as the point number, so one label carries through from the 
instrument to the map and the report. For HeatMap Builder a label of no more than 3 characters is recommended; 
a numeric “level – line – position” code is convenient: 235 means level 2, line 3, position 5. The software also 
generates the point number table automatically from the loaded files – numbers from 1 up to the number of 
files – and exports it for editing: the numbers can be changed while keeping the file structure, and the table loaded 
back.
Figure 2 – Automatic generation of the point number table
3.3 Metrics by point
The specialist sets the temperature and humidity range limits according to the acceptance criteria of their 
methodology. For each point the software calculates over the selected interval:
• minimum, maximum and mean values;
• maximum variation – the difference between the maximum and the minimum;
• date and time of the recorded extreme value;
• risk level of exceeding the upper and the lower range limit.
In the model table, values above the upper limit are highlighted in red and values below the lower limit in blue.

3.4 Separate periods, zones and seasons
The calculation interval is set by date and time; by default it covers the whole data period. This allows separate 
segments to be taken from one mission: stable operation after reaching the set point, door-opening, power-failure 
or defrost tests during chamber qualification. Each segment is calculated as its own model, with its own statistics, 
risk levels and maps.
Zones with different conditions, for example +15…+25 °C and +2…+8 °C, are processed as separate models: each 
model takes the files of its own zone, and the range limits are set for that zone.
The Guideline recommends at least two seasonal studies (clause 14). With an unchanged placement scheme and 
labels, exports from seasonal and repeat studies are compared point by point.

3.6 Critical points
The software identifies high- and medium-risk points and exports them as a separate table with critical 
temperature and humidity values. The table feeds the results section of the report, corrective action records and 
the justification of routine monitoring sensor locations.

3.7 Charts
The software builds four types of chart:
• general temperature chart – all points on one plot;
• general relative humidity chart;
• histogram of minimum, mean and maximum values by point;
• histogram of maximum variations.
Range limits can be shown on the charts as an option. Selecting a curve displays the logger number, date, time and 
value – this is how the moment of an excursion is found. The chart settings define the axis range and auto-scaling, 
axis labels, histogram bar width, legend, colours and style. Charts are saved as PNG; the general chart and its 
legend are saved as separate files, which simplifies report layout.

3.8 Temperature and humidity maps
The map background is a room plan in PNG format. The specialist outlines the modelling zone and places logger 
strings – vertical lines of points with up to four levels; levels are numbered from the bottom up. Each point number 
is placed on the map once: used numbers are removed from the list. For the selected level the software builds a 
colour field of temperature or humidity based on maximum, mean or minimum values, with an adjustable scale 
and legend. For rack storage and chambers with shelves a separate map is built for each level. Maps are saved as 
PNG and can be printed.
Figure 6 – Warehouse map with racks: string numbers, point numbers on two levels, lower-level values

5. Remote qualification via the Bereg-Online gateway
The Bereg-Online gateway brings BEREG data loggers into a web interface: the customer’s specialist prepares 
missions, monitors the study and retrieves logger reports in any format – BEREG, CSV, PDF – from a workstation at 
the head office. Physical operations remain on site: installing and removing loggers according to the scheme 
provided. The gateway is ordered as a separate item.
The gateway communicates with the data loggers via Bluetooth, so it is placed close to the object. In permanent 
connection mode up to 20 loggers are bound to the gateway – the complete set for a small cold room or a 
refrigerated truck body stays connected from placement to removal. The gateway installation point for a specific 
chamber or truck body is determined together with the manufacturer’s engineers.

In warehouses and other zones where loggers are spread beyond the communication range, the gateway works at

the web interface from the head office is agreed with the manufacturer’s engineers before ordering.
The remote approach suits periodic requalification of objects in stable operation. Initial qualification of a new 
object is usually performed with a specialist on site.

6. Data integrity and validation
The software is designed to operate within the customer’s quality system: raw data are kept in their original form, 
the calculation logic is documented and the result is reproducible.
• Original source. The software opens logger files read-only: the original BEREG and CSV files remain in their 
original form and are available for independent re-checking. BEREG logger data are processed directly from 
the binary memory image of the instrument.
• Reproducibility. Reprocessing the same files with the same range limits gives an identical result on any 
workstation with the software.
• Traceability. The logger identification number and the point number accompany the data from the original 
file to tables, charts and maps. The BEREG logger label is shown in reports, in the Bereg-Online gateway web 
interface and over Bluetooth – next to the MAC address when a smartphone connects; during mapping it is 
usually also written on the logger tag. In remote qualification each installation point is confirmed by lighting 
the logger LED before the mission starts.
• Documented calculation logic. The risk level formulas are given in the operating manual; calculation 
algorithms are provided to the customer on request for software validation.
• Final record. The final record is the mapping report approved in the customer’s quality system; the 
evidence base is the archive of original logger files and software exports.

6.2 Software validation
The software is subject to validation within the customer’s quality system as a computerised system processing 
GxP data. The GAMP 5 category – 3 or 4 – is determined by the customer according to its own procedure. Typical 
scope: installation qualification (IQ) – installation on the workstation, recording of the software version and 
licence; operational qualification (OQ) – processing of a reference data set and comparison with an independent 
calculation. The combined Excel table is convenient for such a comparison.
7. Metrological basis
The metrological basis of mapping is formed by the data loggers – measuring instruments of approved type: the 
specialist selects a version whose error meets the criteria of their methodology; the software performs 
calculations on the logger data. Verification details of the loggers are stated in the report (clauses 16(a) and 20(l) of 
the Guideline). The Guideline recommends data loggers with a recording interval from 1 to 15 min or more, an 
absolute error not exceeding ±0.5 °C, with a verification mark and/or a verification certificate (clause 9).

On first start the software displays the computer code. The customer sends the code to the manufacturer together 
with the company name and taxpayer ID (INN), city, full name of the responsible person, phone number and work

software displays the licence status and its validity period. When the computer is replaced, the key is reissued on 
request to the manufacturer.
